Most hosting comparison pages show a very low monthly price. Look closer and you will usually find three conditions attached to it.

## 1. The price needs a long prepay

The headline number is often only available if you pay for two, three or four years upfront. Choose monthly billing and the same plan can cost several times more.

## 2. The renewal price is much higher

When the first term ends, the plan renews at its "regular" price. On popular shared and entry cloud plans, public list prices show renewal rates of roughly **three to four times** the intro price.

## 3. The work is still yours

Cheap plans are usually self-managed. Updates, plugin conflicts, backups you can actually restore, SSL problems and migrations are left to you or your developer, and that time has a cost too.

## What to check before you buy

- **The renewal price**, not the intro price. Multiply it by 12 for the true yearly cost.
- **The term** needed to get the advertised price.
- **What "managed" means.** Who applies updates? Who restores a backup? How fast?
- **Per-seat fees** if developers need access.
- **Usage meters** for bandwidth, requests or build minutes.
